An agent-run study of Insight Timer: every public feature mapped, 2,000 real App Store reviews read and ranked, and each finding placed where it fits, or does not fit, in Aurelia’s first version.

13 November 2026 · Method: replica-skill (recon and entrepreneur steps), run by an AI agent, no manual work · Sources listed at the end

The short version

Five things worth remembering if you read nothing else.

1. They are building our idea in public.Insight Timer’s new “Map of Healing” links practices to body changes to the people they help. That is Aurelia’s causal graph, told as a story. We should tell ours first, and better.
2. Their users are angry at exactly what we were about to copy.Questions before listening, streak pop-ups, a paywall on rewind. In 2025 nearly one review in four was 1 or 2 stars, up from one in eleven in 2022; 2026 is still about one in five.
3. Our daily check-in is the one risk.Asking “how do you feel?” on every sign-in is the exact pattern their reviewers hate. Once a day, skippable, remembered.
4. What people love is not size.It is “free”, “something for my mood”, and “it became my daily habit”. Aurelia can win the second and third without a library of 350,000.
5. Three small things belong in v1.A crisis “Need help now?” link, topic words as starting points, and never paywalling rewind or resume.

What Insight Timer is

The core loop, in their own words: open the app, pick a free practice from a huge library, listen, come back tomorrow.

It started fifteen years ago as a simple meditation timer with bells. Today it calls itself the world’s most popular wellness app: 350,000 practices, more than 100 new ones a day, live sessions every hour, 125,000 therapists sharing it with clients, and one optional subscription. Its homepage promise is three words long: “Free. No Ads. No AI.”

That last word matters for us. Aurelia’s whole product is a session made by AI for one person. Insight Timer is positioning against that, and a few of its users (a small theme so far) already complain about the AI it has started to add.

Strategic note: the Map of Healing is our graph, told well

Insight Timer describes “billions of pathways between who a person is, what they practise, and what changes in the body”, in layers: practices → mechanisms → body changes → benefits → the people they help, plus the person’s life events, traits and how they like a practice delivered. Every link is “a count of documented connections, never a claim of evidence strength.”

That is very close to Aurelia’s 2025 Wellness State Graph, and to the “Lego” changes in a session (Longer exhale, Steady pulse, Fewer words), which are the dots of our causal network. Two differences are ours to claim:

  • They map a library; we test on one person. Their map says what tends to help people like you. Each Aurelia chapter is a small experiment that shows what helped you, and feeds it back.
  • They count connections; we look for state flips. Our model aims at the moment someone moves from one state to a better one, and at what caused it.

Recommendation: give the graph a public name and a one-screen explainer before launch, using the State-Flip film. Without it, Aurelia will look like “AI-generated meditations” next to a competitor that owns the science story.

Three ways to position Aurelia against it

Recommended · For people who want help, not a library

For people who open a meditation app and scroll past a hundred choices without feeling better, Aurelia makes one session for how you feel today, and learns from each one what actually helps you.

Evidence: “cannot find things” and “too many choices” in 36 of the 168 angry 2026 reviews; “something for my mood” in 310 five-star ones.

For people tired of being gamified

No streaks, no milestones, no questions before you listen. You say how you feel; Aurelia does the rest.

Evidence: questions and pop-ups are the largest theme (65 of the 168 angry 2026 reviews), streaks another 27.

For people who want to know why it works

Every change Aurelia suggests says why, and your own check-ins show whether it did.

Evidence: AI-transparency and content-vetting complaints; the Map of Healing shows the market wants the “why”.

What to do with this

Into version 1 (small, before real users)

  1. “Need help now?” link in the drawer, Settings and the check-in sheet, to a page of crisis lines by country.
  2. Daily check-in at most once a day, skippable, remembered; never on every sign-in once accounts exist.
  3. Topic words as starting points on Create and Discover: Sleep, Anxiety, Overthinking, Burnout, Nervous system, Grief.
  4. Rule in the PRD: rewind, resume and offline replay of your own sessions are never paid features.
  5. Say “made with AI” plainly, next to the “Why?” on each change.

Version 1.1 and later

  • A “just breathe” timer mode with bells, no voice, works offline.
  • A public explainer of the causal graph (name, one screen, the State-Flip film).
  • Expert-written “Lego” blocks with a name on them.
  • A therapist channel: share a session by link or QR, starting with two or three therapists in the feedback study.
  • Write mindful minutes to Apple Health from the mobile app.

Method, sources and limits

Run by an AI agent following replica-skill’s recon and entrepreneur steps (MIT licence). Public pages and official feeds only: no account, no login, no paywall crossed, no app code read. Pages were read one at a time, not crawled.

  • Website: home, science (Map of Healing), health, courses, challenges, live, therapists, retreats, teachers, help now, one track page.
  • App Store listing: description, premium price, release notes.
  • 2,000 reviews from Apple’s public customer-reviews feed, the most recent 500 in each of AU, CA, GB, US, 2022-03-22 to 2026-11-12. Every quote above is copied exactly; long ones are shortened with “…”. Apple’s feed gives no per-review link, so “source” opens that country’s review list.

Limits. One review source (App Store), English-speaking stores only; no Google Play, Reddit or Trustpilot yet. Theme counts use keyword matching and are directional, not exact. The mobile app itself was not opened (it is not available in our region); everything about it comes from the listing and reviews. Nothing here is a measurement of Aurelia’s own users.
