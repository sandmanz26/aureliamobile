The feedback group: does Aurelia actually help people get better?

Thirty people, each with one issue Aurelia is meant to help with, will use it for eight weeks. This document explains why we run the study, how it is designed, what data we must collect, what counts as success, and the new Experimentation section in admin where the team tracks it.

In one paragraph. So far we have tested whether people understand and come back to Aurelia: a simulation with six AI personas, then three real interviews. We have not tested whether it helps. This study does that in the simplest honest way. Thirty people with one target issue each (bad sleep, anxiety, mental fog, low energy, poor focus, a stressed body) use Aurelia for eight weeks. Every two weeks they answer a short, widely used well-being questionnaire (WHO-5), and at the start, middle and end a validated questionnaire for their own issue. The app records how often they actually use it. Before we start we fix the pass lines, so the result cannot be read to fit. The study can show whether people improve while using Aurelia and whether they keep using it, which is enough to decide whether the MVP deserves a proper controlled trial. It cannot prove that Aurelia caused the change, and we will not claim that it did.

1. Why this study, and why now

The brief: a group of 30 people with different issues tries Aurelia for a period of time, to see whether they can "solve and win them": bad sleep to good sleep, mental fog to mental clarity. The admin needs a section to track and measure the group, as validation of therapeutic outcomes for the MVP.

The study fills the gap between "people like it" and "it helps". It is also the first time real use is measured over weeks. That is why the work to keep people signed in (decision D2) becomes a must-have before week 0.

The meaningful-change figures are the thresholds commonly used in the research literature for these instruments. A clinical advisor should confirm them before week 0, together with licensing: WHO-5, PHQ-9, GAD-7 and PROMIS are free to use; ISI and PSS-10 may need permission for commercial use. Sleep and calm are the largest cohorts because they had the largest shares in the simulated panel. That is an assumption about early users, not a measurement.

6. What counts as success

Set before week 0 and not changed afterwards. Outcome lines are judged only at week 8; the admin shows interim figures as "not yet".

QuestionPass lineWhy this line
Does well-being improve meaningfully?≥ 50% of people gain 10+ WHO-5 points by week 810 points is the change the WHO-5's authors call meaningful; half the group is a strong signal for a pilot
Does their own issue improve?≥ 40% reach the meaningful change on their cohort measureIssue measures move more slowly than general well-being

7. How we will read the results

  • Responder rates with their uncertainty. With 30 people, a 50% responder rate has a 95% range of roughly 32–68%, so we report the range, not just the number.
  • Change from baseline on WHO-5 and each cohort measure: the average change, a paired test (Wilcoxon signed-rank), and an effect size.
  • Cohorts are described, not tested. With 4–6 people each, a cohort result is a direction, not a finding.
  • Everyone counts: people who withdraw stay in the denominator ("intention to treat"). We also report the result for people who used it as suggested.
  • Use is related to change: do people who played more improve more? This is the closest a single-arm study gets to a dose signal.
  • What we will not claim: that Aurelia caused the change, that it treats any condition, or any number in marketing. People who join a study to feel better often do; regression to the mean and expectation both push scores up. A controlled trial is the next step if the pass lines are met.

8. Safety, consent and privacy

  • Informed consent before any data is stored: what is collected, who sees it, the right to withdraw at any time, and that Aurelia is not a treatment. Anyone in treatment keeps it; Aurelia is never a replacement.
  • Who should not join: people in active crisis, with recent psychiatric hospitalisation, or scoring 20+ on PHQ-9 at screening. They are offered support resources instead.
  • Risk answers go to a person: a PHQ-9 item 9 above zero, a WHO-5 of 28 or below, or a reported adverse event goes to the study lead, who calls the same day. Aurelia never handles it on its own, and the participant never sees a PHQ-9 score.
  • Privacy: pseudonymous ids in every tool, journal text never leaves the participant's account without consent, and wearable data only with a separate consent.
  • Review: an independent ethics review and a clinical advisor before week 0 are strongly recommended. They are needed if the results are ever to be published or used with partners.
