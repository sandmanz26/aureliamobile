1. Why this matters

This is the product-market-fit test for Aurelia. Everything built so far rests on one promise: that a person with a common wellness problem can describe it in a sentence and get better with sessions made for them. Until real people with real problems get better, that promise is a design. In eight weeks we will know whether it holds, for whom, and what has to change.

People solve their issue

They move from the problem range into the healthy range of their own questionnaire. That is a win, and it is what the study is for.

They keep using it

Three or more sessions a week without being pushed harder each week. An effect without use is noise; use without effect is a finding.

They would miss it

At least 40% would be “very disappointed” to lose Aurelia: the usual product-market-fit bar.

The hypothesis. People with a common wellness problem who use Aurelia three or more times a week, with light daily guidance, move from the problem range into the healthy range of their own issue within eight weeks, and would be very disappointed to lose it. What would disprove it: people do not use it even with your guidance, or they use it and do not change, or they change and would not miss it.

2. Your role

You are the guide, Aurelia does the work

Your job is to keep people playing, listening and honest about how they feel. The sessions, the chapters and the changes come from Aurelia. When you are tempted to give advice, ask a question instead and pass what you heard to the product team.

You are the study's memory

Every contact goes in the coaching log the same day. If it is not in the admin, it did not happen, and nobody can learn from it.

You are not a clinician

You never interpret a score to a participant, never suggest stopping a treatment, and never handle a crisis alone. Safety goes to the study lead the same day.

Each milestone has a gate: the lines that say it is passed. The admin computes the same gates in Version 2 → Milestones, so you never have to work them out by hand.

M0

Set up and recruit

Three weeks before week 0

Thirty people across six issues, consented and screened, with the study tools ready.

You

  • Recruit 4–6 people per issue (table in section 3), from outside the team and outside anyone's friends.
  • Screen with PHQ-9 and the exclusion list; anyone excluded is offered support resources, not dropped silently.
  • Take informed consent; give each person a pseudonymous id (P01–P30) and keep the name key outside every tool.
  • Book every baseline call and agree each person's daily time (for sleep, the evening).

Gate: passed when

  • 30 enrolled, every cohort filled
  • Ethics review and clinical advisor: confirmed by a person

If it is not: Do not start with fewer than 24. Extend recruitment a week rather than start a cohort with one person.

M1

Baseline and first session

Week 0

Everyone measured before they start, and playing within two days.

You

  • Collect WHO-5, the cohort measure and PHQ-9 before the first session. A baseline after use is not a baseline.
  • Interview 1 (30 minutes): what the problem is like, what they have tried, what "solved" would mean to them, in their words.
  • Sit with each person (call or in person) for their first session: onboarding, the first sentence, the first play.
  • Write their goal sentence in the coaching log: it is what you will measure the next eight weeks against.

The product team

  • Watches the first sessions for blockers and fixes the ones that stop people playing.

Gate: passed when

  • Baseline WHO-5 and cohort measure: 100%
  • Played a first session in week 1: 90% or more

If it is not: Anyone without a baseline does not count for outcomes. Collect it before they play again.

See it in admin →
M2

Build the habit

Weeks 1–2

A daily rhythm: three or more sessions a week, a check-in after each.

You

  • One short message a day at each person's agreed time (templates in section 6). Ask, do not lecture.
  • Anyone under two sessions in a week gets a call: what got in the way, and one small change (shorter, earlier, a different voice).
  • Log every contact in the participant's coaching log, the same day.
  • Collect WHO-5 at the end of week 2.

The product team

  • A weekly 30-minute review with you: the top three blockers, ranked by how many people they hit.

Gate: passed when

  • 3+ sessions in both weeks 1 and 2: 70% or more
  • WHO-5 week 2 answered: 85% or more

If it is not: Below 70% the study measures who had time, not whether Aurelia helps. Fix the blocker before week 3, even if it is not in the app.

M3

Midpoint read, first iteration

Weeks 3–4

Read what is and is not working, and ship one change for the second half.

You

  • Week 4: WHO-5, cohort measure, PHQ-9 and interview 2 with everyone.
  • Bring the top three findings to the product team, each with the participants it affects and their own words.
  • When the change ships, explain it to participants in one message: what changed and why.

The product team

  • Ships one change, as a new design version on staging first, by the start of week 5.
  • Does not change the questionnaires or the timing: the measures must stay comparable.

Gate: passed when

  • Still enrolled: 85% or more
  • Due questionnaires collected: 85% or more
  • Iteration shipped to the group: confirmed by the product team

If it is not: Interim outcome figures are shown but not judged. A disappointing week 4 is information for the iteration, not a verdict.

M4

Second half, second iteration

Weeks 5–7

Measure whether the change helped, and make one more adjustment if needed.

You

  • Keep the daily rhythm. People who are winning need less; people who are stuck need a call a week.
  • Collect WHO-5 at week 6.
  • Follow up every safety alert the same day, and write down what you did.

The product team

  • Compares weeks 5–7 with weeks 1–4 for the people the change was for.
  • A second change only if the first did not help, again on staging first.

Gate: passed when

  • Adherent people (3+ sessions in 60% of weeks): 60% or more
  • Every safety alert followed up the same day: confirmed by the study lead

If it is not: If adherence falls in the second half, ask why in a call before changing anything else.

M5

Final read and decision

Weeks 8–9

The pass lines decide: go, iterate or pivot.

You

  • Week 8: WHO-5, cohort measure, PHQ-9, the value question and interview 3.
  • Write one story per cohort: where the person started, what changed, in their words, with their consent.
  • Present the result in week 9 against the pass lines set before week 0.

The product team

  • Prepares the decision: what scales, what changes, what stops.

Gate: passed when

  • Well-being improves: 50% gain 10+ WHO-5 points
  • Their own issue improves: 40% reach the meaningful change
  • People stay: 70% still enrolled
  • Data to judge: 85% collected
  • Would miss it: 40% "very disappointed"

If it is not: The lines were fixed before week 0. Moving them after reading the data turns the study into a story.

7. Testing and iterating with the product team

The study is not only a measurement; it is how the product gets better while people use it. One loop, every week, and one significant change per half so its effect can be read.

  1. HearCoaching log, interviews, check-ins.
  2. RankHow many people it hits × how much it stops them.
  3. ChangeOne change, as a new version on staging first.
  4. ExplainTell participants what changed and why.
  5. MeasureSame measures, before and after the change.

Rules for a change

  • At most one significant change in each half (M3, then M4 if needed).
  • Staging first, as a new design version, then the group.
  • The questionnaires and their timing never change.
  • Every change is written down with the date it reached the group.

What a good finding looks like

“Four of six in the sleep cohort stop the session halfway because the voice is too fast (P02, P04, P05, P06; coaching log 3–5 Nov).” Who, how many, their words, where it is written.

9. Rules that never bend

Privacy

  • Pseudonymous ids (P01–P30) everywhere; names stay outside every tool.
  • No names or contact details in the coaching log.
  • Journal and wearable data only with their own consent.

Safety

  • PHQ-9 item 9 above zero, WHO-5 of 28 or below, or any report of harm: the study lead calls the same day.
  • Crisis resources first, always.
  • Aurelia is not a treatment and never replaces one.

Honesty

  • Pass lines are fixed before week 0.
  • People who leave stay in the count.
  • No claim that Aurelia caused a change: there is no control group.

10. The decision at week 9

Go

Most pass lines met, including "their own issue improves" and "would miss it".

Plan a controlled trial and open a wider beta for the cohorts that won.

Iterate

People use it and stay, but outcomes are short of the lines.

A second eight-week cycle on the same design, with the changes the data points to.

Pivot

People do not use it even with daily guidance, or would not miss it.

Stop and rethink the core loop before more building.

Appendix · The study protocol

The protocol of 10 November, in full: why the study, its design, the measures, the pass lines, how results are read, safety, and what must be built before week 0. The playbook above says how to run it; this says what it is. Sections are numbered P1 to P11.

Pass line for well-being≥ 50%Design improve by 10+ points

In one paragraph. So far we have tested whether people understand and come back to Aurelia: a simulation with six AI personas, then three real interviews. We have not tested whether it helps. This study does that in the simplest honest way. Thirty people with one target issue each (bad sleep, anxiety, mental fog, low energy, poor focus, a stressed body) use Aurelia for eight weeks. Every two weeks they answer a short, widely used well-being questionnaire (WHO-5), and at the start, middle and end a validated questionnaire for their own issue. The app records how often they actually use it. Before we start we fix the pass lines, so the result cannot be read to fit. The study can show whether people improve while using Aurelia and whether they keep using it, which is enough to decide whether the MVP deserves a proper controlled trial. It cannot prove that Aurelia caused the change, and we will not claim that it did.

P7. How we will read the results

  • Responder rates with their uncertainty. With 30 people, a 50% responder rate has a 95% range of roughly 32–68%, so we report the range, not just the number.
  • Change from baseline on WHO-5 and each cohort measure: the average change, a paired test (Wilcoxon signed-rank), and an effect size.
  • Cohorts are described, not tested. With 4–6 people each, a cohort result is a direction, not a finding.
  • Everyone counts: people who withdraw stay in the denominator ("intention to treat"). We also report the result for people who used it as suggested.
  • Use is related to change: do people who played more improve more? This is the closest a single-arm study gets to a dose signal.
  • What we will not claim: that Aurelia caused the change, that it treats any condition, or any number in marketing. People who join a study to feel better often do; regression to the mean and expectation both push scores up. A controlled trial is the next step if the pass lines are met.

P8. Safety, consent and privacy

  • Informed consent before any data is stored: what is collected, who sees it, the right to withdraw at any time, and that Aurelia is not a treatment. Anyone in treatment keeps it; Aurelia is never a replacement.
  • Who should not join: people in active crisis, with recent psychiatric hospitalisation, or scoring 20+ on PHQ-9 at screening. They are offered support resources instead.
  • Risk answers go to a person: a PHQ-9 item 9 above zero, a WHO-5 of 28 or below, or a reported adverse event goes to the study lead, who calls the same day. Aurelia never handles it on its own, and the participant never sees a PHQ-9 score.
  • Privacy: pseudonymous ids in every tool, journal text never leaves the participant's account without consent, and wearable data only with a separate consent.
  • Review: an independent ethics review and a clinical advisor before week 0 are strongly recommended. They are needed if the results are ever to be published or used with partners.
