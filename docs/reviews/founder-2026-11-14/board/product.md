# Product lens
Score: 6/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## Version scores

| Version | Score | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 3/10 | A new user's first chat is someone else's ("Good morning, Adam… improved by 7%"), there is no onboarding and Progress shows sample percentages. The first five minutes are not about the user. |
| 2 | 6/10 | Onboarding and a first session built from the user's own words fix the opening. Progress still shows invented analytics ("60%, Great Progress!") on day one. |

## Best first version

Version 3. It is the only version where every screen a new user sees in the first session is about that user, from their own sentence through to an honest empty Progress screen. The changes came out of three real interviews, so it is also the version that has already started listening to users. Before real users see it, cut it down further (see conditions).

## What is strong

- **The one thing is clear and good.** You say how you feel in one sentence and get a session made for that moment. That beats scrolling a library of 350,000 tracks, and it answers what Insight Timer's 1-2 star reviewers complain about.
- **The opening experience was designed from the moment of use and worked back.** The pre-filled sentence becomes the first brief. Onboarding takes three skippable questions. "Thank you — that is enough to start."
- **The craft is consistent.** One type scale, one palette and a calm visual tone run across all three versions. The empty states in v3 are written with care.
- **"Listen, say how it felt, the next chapter changes" is a loop people can understand.** It is the reason to come back.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **The core moment has never happened.** There is no backend, so no session has ever been generated, voiced, scored and streamed from one sentence. The whole product rests on that experience, and nobody has heard it. If the audio is generic, slow to arrive or sounds machine-made, everything around it is decoration. Time-to-first-sound and AI cost per session are unknown.
2. **Too many things for one product.** Daily check-in sheet, five-chapter journey, journal, wearables, community stories, Recreate/publishing, coins, referrals, version history, Social Impact tab, Analysis, a two-week report. Each one is reasonable on its own. Together they bury the one thing. The check-in appears on every sign-in, even for a new user who has no "yesterday" yet, and that is exactly the kind of interruption Insight Timer users already complain about.
3. **Seams that break trust in v3 itself.** The About page still shows "↑ 12%" and "↑ 8%" badges, although v3 promises no percentages. Signed-in Home lists "Recent" sessions under other people's names. The chat suggests "Good morning, how did I sleep?" before any wearable is connected. Quick Start cards misspell "exprience". The Upgrade screen sells "more access" and "priority placement in Explore", not a better night's sleep. In a wellness product, invented numbers are what ends trust.

## The questions I would ask the founder

- In one sentence, what does a person hear within 60 seconds of typing their first line, and how long until it plays?
- What does one generated session cost you, and what does the free tier of 5 builds a day cost per user per month?
- If you could ship only the sentence, the session and "how do you feel now?", what would you lose?
- Who has listened to a generated session end to end, and what did they say about the voice and the script?
- Why does a brand-new user get a daily check-in asking about yesterday's session?
- Who is the user that wants community, coins and publishing, and is that the same person who cannot sleep at 1 a.m.?
- What does the 30-person study measure as success, and what result would make you stop?

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. **Make the core real first.** Use one problem (sleep) and one voice, generate real sessions end to end, and measure time to first sound and cost per session before any more interface work.
2. **Put v3 in front of the 30-person study, cut to the loop:** sentence, session, "how do you feel now?", next chapter. Switch off community, coins, publishing, Social Impact and the Daily check-in on first sign-in.
3. **Remove every invented figure a user can see**, including the version-history percentages, and fix the v3 seams listed above.
4. **Rewrite Upgrade around the outcome** (better sleep) and set the price before the study ends, so the study also tests willingness to pay.
5. **Write down one result the study must hit** (for example, a self-reported improvement in sleep over eight weeks) and treat missing it as a reason to rethink, not to add features.
