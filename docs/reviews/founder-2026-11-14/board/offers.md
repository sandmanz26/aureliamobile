# Offers lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## Version scores

| Version | Score | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 3 | A new user's chat opens on someone else's conversation ("Good morning, Adam… score improved by 7%") and Progress shows percentages they never earned. That damages the belief that it will work for *them* before they have built anything. |
| 2 | 6 | Lower effort: three skippable questions, then a first session built from the person's own words. But Progress still shows sample analytics ("60%, Great Progress!") to someone who has not listened yet. |
| 3 | 7 | The most honest offer. Progress is empty until you act ("Your progress starts with your first session"), every change has a "Why?", and the Home promise ("Sessions made for how you feel today") is a buyer outcome, not a feature. |

## Best first version

Version 3. On this lens, believability is where offers die, and Version 3 is the only one that never shows a new user a result they did not produce. It also keeps Version 2's low-effort start: one sentence in and a first session out.

## What is strong

- **The pain is real and frequent.** "I can't fall asleep, my mind keeps racing" is a pain people feel tonight. They do not need to be persuaded they have it. How big the market is and whether it is growing are not in the brief, so both are open questions.
- **Time to first result is short.** You type one sentence and the session gets built, so the buyer does not hunt through a library. That speaks directly to the effort complaints in Aurelia's own study of Insight Timer reviews (questions before listening, streak pop-ups).
- **Some of the stack is cheap to deliver.** Keeping every build in the conversation, the "+" sheet summary before you apply a change, the journal and the (.ics) calendar reminder are mostly software, so they cost little per user once built. The cost per AI session is not stated, so the main item in the stack is unpriced.
- **There is a proof engine in the plan.** The 30-person, eight-week study on one problem (bad sleep to good sleep) is exactly the evidence this offer lacks.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **No proof, at a price matched against a free giant.** $10 a month sits a cent above Insight Timer's US$9.99, and Insight Timer gives away 350,000 practices for free. At the moment the Upgrade screen sells inputs ("More AI sessions", "More room to explore", "Priority placement in Explore") and not an outcome. There is no guarantee and no result shown. A buyer has no reason to believe that paying gets them sleep.
2. **The core claim is unproven and could be expensive to deliver.** The adaptive engine (the Wellness State Graph and its feedback loop) is being reworked, and there is no backend. If a generated session is not clearly better than a good library track, the offer turns into a commodity. If each session costs real money to generate, 30 builds a day for $10 may lose money on heavy users. Both are open questions.
3. **Effort creeps back in.** A daily check-in sheet on every sign-in, onboarding questions, a journal, a wearable prompt and a feedback loop each ask the buyer for a little more. Together they rebuild the "questions before listening" friction that drives competitors' one-star reviews.

## The questions I would ask the founder

- Who exactly pays? Of the people who cannot sleep, which ones have money and have already paid for a fix? Where do you reach the first 100, and what does each one cost? No channel is set yet.
- What is the one outcome you would put your name on, in what time frame? For example: "fall asleep faster within 14 nights".
- What is the guarantee, and can you afford it? A refund if the two-week report shows no change?
- What does one generated session cost to make, and what does a Plus user on 30 builds a day cost you each month?
- Could you charge $20 and still be the obvious choice? If not, what is missing from the stack?
- Is "builds a day" the right limit? Would buyers pay for a result (a sleep programme) rather than a quota?

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. **Launch Version 3, narrowed to one outcome (sleep)**, with the paid offer named and sold as that outcome, not as "more sessions".
2. **Finish the 30-person study before charging**, and put its real results (words and numbers from those people, clearly labelled) on the Upgrade screen. Remove every sample figure from the paid path.
3. **Add a guarantee tied to the two-week report**, priced with the CFO once the cost per session is known.
4. **Cut asks before listening**: the check-in should not interrupt every sign-in, and nothing should come between the first sentence and the first session.
5. **Know the cost per session**, and set the free and Plus limits so that a heavy user does not lose money.
