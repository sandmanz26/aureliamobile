# Creator-led and coach-led distribution economics for Retreat-in-a-Box

Research notes collected 2026-11-23 to test the "Retreat-in-a-Box" assumptions: €19.99 ticket, 8 weeks, cohorts of up to 200, 50 creators, a 60/40 creator/Aurelia split, 30% graduate conversion to Plus ($10/mo or $100/yr), and 76.5% still active at week 8.

Labels used on every finding: **[MEASURED]** means a peer-reviewed or methodologically described measurement. **[COMPANY]** means a platform's own published terms or claim (terms are reliable; performance claims are not audited). **[THIRD-PARTY]** means a review, affiliate or blog site. **[ESTIMATE]** means our own arithmetic or inference.

---

## 1. How creator and teacher marketplaces pay creators (wellness apps and creator platforms)

### Takeaway
Two very different models exist. **Wellness apps (Insight Timer) pay a pooled share of subscription revenue by play count**, and they do not publish the percentage. **Creator-tool platforms (Kajabi, Teachable, Thinkific, Circle, Skool, Patreon, Maven) let the creator keep 80–100% of each sale** and charge 0–10% plus card processing (about 2.9% + $0.30). On these platforms the creator owns the customer and brings the traffic. No reviewed platform that relies on creator-sourced buyers keeps 40%.

Teachers whose content has a high next-day "return rate" get bonus payments. The help page publishes **no percentage**. - [THIRD-PARTY] **Skool**: Hobby $9/mo with a **10% + $0.30** fee per transaction (launched about July 2025). - [THIRD-PARTY] **Kajabi**: no revenue share on Kajabi Payments. Processing is 2.9%/2.8%/2.7% + $0.30 on Basic/Growth/Pro (US). - [THIRD-PARTY] **Thinkific**: no platform transaction fee on Thinkific Payments (2.9% + $0.30). Plans from $54/mo (Basic), or $40 billed annually. — [Ruzuku platform transaction fees](https://www.ruzuku.com/learn/articles/platform-transaction-fees); [Dupple comparison](https://dupple.com/blog/online-course-platforms-comparison)

### Inferences
- [ESTIMATE] A 60/40 split is **far more platform-favourable** than creator tools (0–10% take) and only roughly comparable to the claimed 50% pool share at Insight Timer. Insight Timer, however, *supplies the audience*. In Retreat-in-a-Box the creator supplies the audience, so creators will compare 60% against the roughly 87–97% they keep selling the same programme on Kajabi, Teachable or Maven. Aurelia has to justify its 40% with what it builds (personalised AI sessions, voice, sound, check-ins), which creators cannot get from a course tool.
- [ESTIMATE] The ticket also carries leakage the spec does not mention. Card processing (about 3% + €0.30, or about 4.5% on a €19.99 ticket), EU VAT on digital services (consumer-country rate, typically about 20%), and **Apple's 30% if the ticket is bought in an iOS app** (Insight Timer's donation terms show Apple taking 30%). On a web sale, net revenue per ticket after VAT and processing is roughly €15.6. The question of whether the 60/40 applies to gross or net needs a decision.
- [ESTIMATE] Gross headline: 50 creators × 200 × €19.99 = €199,900 per full cohort wave. Aurelia's 40% is about €80k gross, or about €62k after an assumed ~22% VAT and processing. This is the ceiling if every cohort fills, which is unlikely (see section 6).

### Gaps
- Insight Timer's actual teacher percentage, and how its paid Retreats split revenue: not published on public pages (the help center points to a "Teacher Income FAQ" inside the dashboard).
- Calm and Headspace royalty or fee figures: no public data found. They are private, bespoke deals.
- No public example found of a platform taking about 40% *when the creator brings the buyers and the platform supplies AI or production*. The closest analogues are Maven's reported 50/50 when *Maven* sources corporate buyers (unconfirmed) and app-store-style 30% cuts.

## 2. What revenue splits are normal

### Takeaway
The split tracks **who brings the customer**. Creator brings the customer: the platform takes 0–10% (creator tools) to 30% (app stores). Platform brings the customer: 50/50 to 70/30 in the platform's favour is common (Insight Timer pool, reportedly about 50%; Maven corporate, reportedly 50/50; affiliate commissions up to 30%).

### Cited Findings
- [COMPANY] Maven, creator-sourced sales: 90/10. — [Maven guidebook](https://help.maven.com/en/articles/12240853-maven-instructor-policy-guidebook)
- [COMPANY] Maven pays affiliates up to 30% commission for enrollments they bring. — [Maven guidebook](https://help.maven.com/en/articles/12240853-maven-instructor-policy-guidebook)
- [COMPANY] Patreon 90/10 for new creators (about 92/8 legacy Pro). — [Patreon help](https://support.patreon.com/hc/en-us/articles/36426991446797)
- [COMPANY] Skool Hobby about 90/10. Teachable Starter 92.5/7.5. Circle 98–99.5% to creator. — [Uscreen](https://www.uscreen.tv/blog/skool-pricing/); [Teachable](https://www.teachable.com/pricing); [Circle](https://circle.so/pricing)
- [THIRD-PARTY] Insight Timer pooled subscription share is said to be about 50% (unverified). — [ChoosingTherapy](https://www.choosingtherapy.com/insight-timer-review/)
- [COMPANY] Apple keeps 30% of in-app digital purchases (as applied to Insight Timer donations). — [Insight Timer help](https://insight-timer.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/67000664874-how-can-teachers-earn-revenue-from-their-work-)

### Inferences
- [ESTIMATE] 60/40 sits between "tool" and "marketplace". It is defensible only if Aurelia also does demand generation (featuring cohorts in-app, paid acquisition) or delivers clearly more value than a course tool. Otherwise expect pushback towards 70/30 or 80/20, or creators asking for a share of Plus conversions they generate.
- [ESTIMATE] A common creator ask is a **recurring share of the subscriptions they drive** (Insight Timer pays on ongoing plays; Patreon is recurring). If graduates convert to Plus, creators will likely want a cut of Plus. The spec should model this.

### Gaps
- No industry survey of AI-content-platform splits found. "Platform supplies AI production" splits are not yet published anywhere we could locate.

## 3. Cohort-based course economics (completion, price, refunds, size)

### Takeaway
High completion figures for cohort courses (75–96%) are **company or advocate claims without published methodology**. Measured self-paced MOOC completion is about 4–13% median. Fee-based certificate tracks reach about 60% median. Price points for premium cohorts are hundreds to thousands of dollars, far above €19.99. Refund windows typically run to the halfway mark (Maven) or 14 days (Reforge).

### Cited Findings
- [COMPANY claim] Maven: "75–90% completion rate" (Product Hunt listing). Another review cites about 96%. Neither gives a methodology. — [Product Hunt: Maven](https://www.producthunt.com/posts/maven-eca0ac99-b076-4f60-8cb7-4d204fe8ff7f); [Dr Philippa Hardman Substack](https://drphilippahardman.substack.com/p/maven-a-masterclass-in-great-online)
- [ADVOCATE claim, 2021] a16z (Andrew Chen) described cohort-based courses as having over 75% completion. — [a16z: Investing in Maven](https://a16z.com/announcement/investing-in-maven/)
- [COMPANY claim, secondary] altMBA reported 96% completion (repeated widely, attributed to altMBA and HBR). We found no primary altMBA source. Others say "north of 90%". — [Cohort Learning Method PDF](https://nanmckayconnects.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Cohort-Learning-Method.pdf); [Learnopoly cohort stats](https://learnopoly.com/cohort-based-learning-statistics/); [Seth Godin, altMBA update (2017)](https://seths.blog/2017/02/altmba-update/)
- [MEASURED, 2014] Ho et al. (HarvardX/MITx): MOOC completion ranged 0.7%–52.1%, **median 12.6%**. — cited in [IRRODL 2019](https://www.erudit.org/fr/revues/irrodl/2019-v20-n3-irrodl04799/1062521ar.pdf)
- [MEASURED, 2013] Breslow et al., MIT physics MOOC: 154,763 registrants, 5% earned the certificate. — [Cunningham dissertation, ASU](https://keep.lib.asu.edu/system/files/c7/179037/Cunningham_asu_0010E_16844.pdf)
- [MEASURED] A self-paced college algebra MOOC (4,600+ participants) had **4%** completion. — [Cunningham dissertation, ASU](https://keep.lib.asu.edu/system/files/c7/179037/Cunningham_asu_0010E_16844.pdf)
- [MEASURED, 2016] Chuang & Ho: the median completion for **fee-paying certificate learners tops out around 60%**. Paying raises completion a lot. — cited in [IRRODL 2019](https://www.erudit.org/fr/revues/irrodl/2019-v20-n3-irrodl04799/1062521ar.pdf)
- [MEASURED] Even learners who *intend* to complete averaged only 22% (Reich 2014). — cited in [IRRODL 2019](https://www.erudit.org/fr/revues/irrodl/2019-v20-n3-irrodl04799/1062521ar.pdf)
- [COMPANY] Maven refunds: full refund until the course's halfway point. After that, case by case. Stripe fees are not refunded. — [Maven guidebook](https://help.maven.com/en/articles/12240853-maven-instructor-policy-guidebook)
- [THIRD-PARTY] Reforge: refunds within 14 days (per Course Report). Pricing undisclosed; one forum post says about $2k. — [Course Report: Reforge](https://coursereport.com/schools/reforge); [Team Blind](https://www.teamblind.com/post/is-reforge-worth-it-shksc0ip)
- [THIRD-PARTY] On Deck programmes ran 8–10 weeks. On Deck Scale cost $10,000/year. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2405954)
- [THIRD-PARTY, weak] Course refund rates: "2–6% for most of the last decade". About 4.8% across creator platforms (attributed to Podia). 5–10% for mid-ticket courses. A claimed 21% in Q1 2026 comes from a vendor selling an alternative and is unsourced. — [Communipass](https://communipass.com/blog/online-course-refund-crisis-2026/); [Voxbooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/digital-course-sales-statistics-2026/); [Learning Revolution refund calculator](https://www.learningrevolution.net/creator-tools/refund-rate-cost-calculator/)
- [COMPANY] Teachable chargeback rate is about 0.18–0.20%, against a cited 0.50% industry average (chargebacks, not refunds). — [Teachable blog](https://www.teachable.com/blog/teachable-payments-chargeback-and-risk-detection)

### Inferences
- [ESTIMATE] The cohort-completion halo comes from **expensive, live, instructor-led, small-group, high-accountability** programmes (altMBA was about $4,000+ and selective). A €19.99, mostly asynchronous AI programme for 200 people resembles a cheap self-paced product far more than altMBA. The honest prior sits between self-paced (about 5–13%) and fee-paying certificate learners (about 60%), not 76.5%.
- [ESTIMATE] Copying Maven's refund-to-midpoint guarantee on an 8-week programme would mean refunds through week 4. Budget 5–10% refunds as a placeholder.

### Gaps
- No audited completion data for Maven, Reforge or On Deck. No public cohort-size distributions (Maven publishes no cohort-size guidance in its policy guide).

## 4. Wellness challenge and programme retention benchmarks (4–8 weeks)

### Takeaway
Measured retention for mental-health and mindfulness apps is very low (**median day-30 retention 3.3% overall, 4.7% for mindfulness apps**). Even Noom's company-funded data shows heavy drop-off in real-world use compared with trials. A **76.5% active at week 8** claim has no support in any public benchmark we found. It would be roughly 15× the measured mindfulness median, and it should be presented as a target, not a forecast.

### Cited Findings
- [MEASURED, 2019] Baumel et al., JMIR, 93 mental-health apps (panel data): **median 15-day retention 3.9%, 30-day 3.3%**. By type at day 30: mindfulness/meditation 4.7%, peer support 8.9%, trackers 6.1%, breathing 0.0%. IQRs are wide. — [Baumel et al. 2019, JMIR](https://www.jmir.org/2019/9/e14567)
- [MEASURED, company-funded] Noom RCT (n=427): 86.7% retained at 68 weeks in the *trial* (control 86.6%). Self-reported weight, funded by Noom. Trial retention is a study-protocol figure, not product engagement. — [PubMed 34747706](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34747706)
- [MEASURED] Engagement with the same digital weight-loss programme was "significantly greater in empirical trials than in real-world" use. — [PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8663454)
- [COMPANY] Noom GLP-1 programme: 43.6% of users engaged with the app at 30 days (internal, unreviewed). — [Noom](https://www.noom.com/?p=26025159)
- [THIRD-PARTY] Les Mills on challenges as a member-retention tool (gym context, qualitative). — [Les Mills research insights](https://www.lesmills.com/clubs-and-facilities/research-insights/acquisition-retention/the-power-of-challenges-to-keep-members-motivated)

### Inferences
- [ESTIMATE] A paid ticket plus a creator's social accountability plus a cohort will raise retention above free-app baselines (the fee-paying MOOC effect: about 60% against about 13%). A defensible planning range for "still active at week 8" might be **20–50%**, with 76.5% as a stretch case. This is our judgement, not a benchmark.
- [ESTIMATE] The spec's 76.5% should be traced to its source. If it comes from a trial-style or Noom-RCT-style figure, it measures study retention (people answering follow-ups), not weekly use.

### Gaps
- No public completion data found for 75 Hard, Insight Timer courses, meditation 30-day challenges or online yoga teacher programmes. These are claimed anecdotally but not measured.

## 5. Conversion from a paid programme or challenge to an ongoing subscription

### Takeaway
**No published benchmark exists for "paid cohort/challenge graduate converts to app subscription".** The nearest anchors are trial-to-paid rates for health and fitness subscriptions (about 27–64%, varying by trial length and source), and those apply to people who opted into a free trial of the *same* product. 30% is plausible for graduates who were active at week 8. It is optimistic if applied to *all* buyers.

### Cited Findings
- [THIRD-PARTY] Health and fitness app trial-to-paid: 39.9% overall, 68.3% in the top decile. Uscreen reports 63.7% for fitness video subscriptions. — summarised in [Zenoti](https://www.zenoti.com/thecheckin/from-free-trials-to-full-membership-using-software-to-convert-more-gym-leads); [Uscreen](https://www.uscreen.tv/?p=68615)
- [THIRD-PARTY] Trial length matters: 17–32-day trials have a median conversion of 45.7%, against 26.8% for 4-day trials. — [Zenoti](https://www.zenoti.com/thecheckin/from-free-trials-to-full-membership-using-software-to-convert-more-gym-leads)
- [THIRD-PARTY] Gyms: 19% trial-to-membership without structured follow-up, 38% with it (attributed to Fitness Business Pro 2024, unverified). — [Rework](https://resources.rework.com/vi/libraries/gym-fitness-growth/free-trial-day-pass-strategies)
- [THIRD-PARTY] Paid fitness leads: only 3–6 in 100 become members. — [Keepme](https://www.keepme.ai/blog/why-only-3-to-6-out-every-100-paid-fitness-leads-become-members)
- [THIRD-PARTY, advice] Make the membership offer about halfway through a 2–4 week studio challenge. — [WellnessLiving](https://www.wellnessliving.com/blog/?p=34156)

### Inferences
- [ESTIMATE] Funnel math matters. 30% × "graduates" depends on the denominator. With 200 buyers, 76.5% active gives 153 graduates and 46 Plus conversions. With a more conservative 35% active, there are 70 graduates; 30% of those is 21 conversions, about 10% of buyers. Report Plus conversion **as a share of buyers** as well as of graduates.
- [ESTIMATE] The creator's audience joined for the creator. Post-programme loyalty may follow the creator, not the app, unless Plus keeps the creator's voice or content present (which argues for a creator share of Plus revenue).

### Gaps
- No public data on programme-to-subscription conversion for meditation or wellness creators, Noom-style programmes or Insight Timer course buyers.

## 6. Creator funnel conversion and audience size needed to fill 200 seats

### Takeaway
There is **no credible published "X% of followers buy" benchmark**. The widely repeated "1%" has no traceable source. Creator sales are driven by email lists more than follower counts, and per-email buy rates are small. Filling 200 seats at €19.99 realistically needs a creator with an engaged email list in the five figures, or a large social following. Many of 50 creators will not fill 200.

### Cited Findings
- [COMPANY case] Kit creator Gannon Meyer: about 9,000 subscribers, 2–3 sales of low-priced products per weekly newsletter (about 0.02–0.03% per send). His bootcamp launch made $50K+ "primarily through email". — [Kit case study](https://kit.com/resources/blog/gannon-meyer-instagram-to-email-automation)
- [COMPANY] Kit cites 40% average email open rates for creators. — [Kit creators page](https://kit.com/creators)
- [MEASURED survey, 2026] Sprout Social: only 17% of consumers check follower count before engaging with a creator. — [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/creator-fit-beats-follower-count-for-brands-heres-the-numbers-say/828421/)
- [THIRD-PARTY] Captiv8: nano-influencers (<50k followers) saw revenue per click up 74%, the biggest rise of any tier. — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/captiv8-affiliate-marketing-report-shows-sector-maturing)
- [THIRD-PARTY] Product and merch sales plus affiliate make up 21.2% of creator income (2026 Creator Economy Report). — [Influencer Marketing Factory via Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/press-release/20260223-the-influencer-marketing-factory-releases-2026-creator-economy-report-highl/)
- [THIRD-PARTY] CreatorIQ: brands say fit matters most, yet the market still pays on follower count. — [CreatorIQ State of Creators](https://www.creatoriq.com/whitepaper/state-of-creators)

### Inferences
- [ESTIMATE, illustrative only] Selling 200 tickets needs about 200 ÷ (buy rate). At 0.5–2% of an engaged email list per launch (a common rule of thumb we could not source), that means 10k–40k engaged subscribers. At a much lower follower-to-buyer rate (0.05–0.2% of social followers), it means 100k–400k followers. Treat these as orders of magnitude to validate with pilot creators, not benchmarks.
- [ESTIMATE] Creator take at full cohort: 200 × €19.99 × 60% ≈ €2,400 gross, or about €1,870 after VAT and processing, per 8-week cohort. For a creator with 50k+ followers, that is small compared with selling their own €99–€299 programme on Kajabi or Maven at 90%+. It is attractive to small creators, but small creators cannot fill 200 seats. **This price × split × size combination may be the core weakness.**

### Gaps
- No sourced funnel benchmarks (followers → email → buyers) for wellness creators. Kit, Linktree and SignalFire reports did not surface usable buy-rate figures in this search window.

## 7. Creator acquisition and retention (what makes creators stay; cold start)

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin. What exists says creators stay where there is **meaningful, predictable income and audience they own**. Insight Timer pays engagement bonuses (next-day return rate) and keeps teachers with free distribution plus donations. Fee hikes cause visible backlash (Patreon 2025). Platforms protect incumbents with legacy rates.

### Cited Findings
- [COMPANY] Insight Timer pays bonuses for content that brings users back the next day, and uses free Live Events as a funnel into the teacher's paid content. — [Insight Timer earnings](https://insight-timer.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/67000664874-how-can-teachers-earn-revenue-from-their-work-); [Live Events](https://insight-timer.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/67000754308-what-are-live-events-)
- [COMPANY] Patreon grandfathered existing creators at 8%/12%/5% and applied 10% only to new pages. Unpublishing a page forfeits legacy rates. — [Patreon help](https://support.patreon.com/hc/en-us/articles/36426991446797); [TubeFilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/06/18/patreon-taking-larger-cut-new-users-earnings-10-percent/)
- [THIRD-PARTY] Maven pivoted in 2022 from creators to "experts", reflecting difficulty sustaining creator-led cohort supply and demand. — [TechCrunch, 2022-09-13](https://techcrunch.com/2022/09/13/mavens-a16z-backed-teaching-platform-pivots-from-creators-to-experts/amp/)

## 8. Physical "box" or kit components

### Takeaway
All-in fulfilment and shipping for a subscription-style box runs about **$8–18 per box before product cost**. That is **40–90% of a €19.99 ticket**, so a physical box is economically incompatible with a €19.99 price and a 60/40 split unless it is sold separately or premium-priced.

### Cited Findings
- [THIRD-PARTY] Most subscription brands spend $8–$15 per box on all-in fulfilment and shipping, before product. Kitting labour and dimensional weight are the main drivers. — [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/checkpoint/best-subscription-box-fulfillment-services/)
- [THIRD-PARTY] Wellness-specific: total fulfilment per order $8.85–$18.25, including $6–$10 ground shipping and $1.50–$4.00 kitting. — [Fulfyld wellness benchmarks](https://www.fulfyld.com/data/wellness-healthcare-fulfillment-benchmarks/)
- [THIRD-PARTY] Average US shipping $4–$8.50 per box, packaging about $1.50. — [Fulfillrite](https://fulfillrite.com/blog/what-does-subscription-box-fulfillment-cost/)
- [THIRD-PARTY] Target at least 40% gross margin at launch (50–60% at scale) after product, packaging, shipping, fees and acquisition. — [BigCommerce](https://www.bigcommerce.com/articles/ecommerce/how-to-start-a-subscription-box-business/)

### Inferences
- [ESTIMATE] For a mid-weight kit (journal, eye mask, tea), product at about $5–10 plus fulfilment at about $9–18 gives $14–28 per box. That is at or above the whole €19.99 ticket. Cross-border EU and UK shipping adds customs and VAT friction. "In-a-Box" should either be digital-only (a branded welcome pack or download) or a separately priced add-on (e.g. €49+).

### Gaps
- No data found on Calm's physical products (retail or cost), Hims-style kit costs, or meditation-kit margins. EU-specific fulfilment benchmarks not found (the figures above are US).
