# Creators and cohorts
*Part of the [B2B2C research](/wiki/b2b2c). Sources: 37, from the notes in `docs/research/research_notes/Aurelia B2B2C models and plan/`.*

This page tests the creator side of Retreat-in-a-Box: a creator sells an 8-week paid digital retreat at €19.99, in cohorts of up to 200, with 50 creators, a 60/40 creator/Aurelia split, 30% of graduates converting to Plus and 76.5% claimed active at week 8. All research was collected on 2026-11-23.

Labels used on this page: **(measured)** is a peer-reviewed or methodologically described measurement. **(company terms)** is a platform's own published price or policy. **(company claim)** is a company's own performance claim, not audited. **(third-party)** is a review, blog or affiliate site. **(estimate)** is our own arithmetic or judgement.

## In one minute

- **The 60/40 split is far richer for the platform than the market.** Creator tools let the creator keep 80–100% of a sale. A 40% platform cut only appears where the platform brings the buyers. In Retreat-in-a-Box the creator brings them.
- **76.5% active at week 8 has no support in any public benchmark.** Measured mindfulness-app retention at day 30 is 4.7%. Fee-paying online learners finish at about 60% at best. Treat 76.5% as a stretch target, not a forecast.
- **No published data exists on paid-programme graduates converting to a subscription.** 30% per graduate is plausible. Applied to all buyers, it is optimistic. See [Unit economics](/wiki/b2b2c/economics) for the per-buyer figures.
- **Filling 200 seats needs a large, engaged audience.** That likely means an email list in the tens of thousands (estimate). Many of 50 creators will not fill 200.
- **A physical box costs about as much as the whole ticket.** Fulfilment alone runs $8–18 per box before the product.
- **The core weakness is price × split × cohort size.** A full cohort earns the creator about €1,900. Large creators can earn more selling their own programme; small creators cannot fill 200 seats.

## How platforms pay creators

Two models exist. Wellness apps pay teachers a pooled share of subscription revenue by plays. Creator-tool platforms let the creator keep almost all of each sale and charge a small fee plus card processing.

## What split is normal

The split follows **who brings the customer**.

| Who brings the buyer | Typical platform share | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The creator | 0–10% (tools) up to 30% (app stores) | Maven 10%, Patreon 10%, Teachable 0–7.5%, Circle 0.5–2%, Apple 30% |
| The platform | About 50% or more | Insight Timer pool (about 50%, third-party); Maven corporate (50/50, unconfirmed) |
| A third party | Up to 30% commission | Maven affiliates |
| **Retreat-in-a-Box (spec)** | **40%, with the creator bringing the buyer** | No public precedent found |

What this means (estimate): creators will compare 60% against the roughly 87–97% they keep selling the same programme on Kajabi, Teachable or Maven. Aurelia has to earn its 40% with what creators cannot get from a course tool: personalised AI sessions, voice, sound and check-ins. Or it has to bring demand itself, through in-app placement or paid acquisition. Without that, expect pressure towards 70/30 or 80/20.

Creators are also likely to ask for a **recurring share of the Plus subscriptions they drive** (estimate). Insight Timer pays on ongoing plays and Patreon is recurring, so the precedent exists. The spec does not model it yet.

## Completion: what the evidence says

High cohort-course completion figures are claims without a published method. Measured completion for self-paced courses is very low. Paying raises it a lot.

| Programme type | Completion | Date | Label | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Maven cohort courses | 75–90% (another review says about 96%) | Undated listing | company claim, no method | [Product Hunt][ph-maven] |
| Cohort-based courses in general | Over 75% | 2021 | advocate claim (investor) | [a16z][a16z] |
| altMBA | 96% ("north of 90%" elsewhere); no primary source found | 2017 and later | company claim, secondary | [Learnopoly][learnopoly]; [Seth Godin][godin] |
| HarvardX/MITx MOOCs | Median 12.6% (range 0.7–52.1%) | 2014 | measured | [IRRODL 2019][irrodl] |
| MIT physics MOOC | 5% of 154,763 earned the certificate | 2013 | measured | [Cunningham, ASU][asu] |
| Self-paced college algebra MOOC | 4% | Not dated | measured | [Cunningham, ASU][asu] |
| Fee-paying certificate learners | Median tops out around 60% | 2016 | measured | [IRRODL 2019][irrodl] |
| Learners who intended to finish | 22% on average | 2014 | measured | [IRRODL 2019][irrodl] |

The high figures come from expensive, live, small-group programmes. altMBA cost about $4,000 or more and was selective (estimate from the notes). A €19.99, mostly asynchronous AI programme for 200 people looks more like a cheap self-paced product. The honest prior sits between about 5–13% (self-paced) and about 60% (fee-paying), not at 76.5% (estimate).

76.5% active at week 8 would be roughly 15 times the measured mindfulness median (estimate). If the figure came from a trial, it measures people answering follow-ups, not weekly use. Its origin should be traced before anyone quotes it.

**Planning range (estimate):** a paid ticket, a creator's accountability and a cohort should lift retention above free-app levels. We use **20–50% active at week 8** for planning, with 76.5% as a stretch case. This is our judgement, not a benchmark.

## Graduate conversion to Plus

No published benchmark exists for a paid cohort or challenge converting to an app subscription. The nearest anchors apply to people who chose a free trial of the same product.

| Anchor | Figure | Label | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Health and fitness app trial-to-paid | 39.9% overall, 68.3% top decile | third-party summary | [Zenoti][zenoti] |
| Trial length: 17–32 days vs 4 days | 45.7% vs 26.8% | third-party summary | [Zenoti][zenoti] |
| Gym trial-to-member without / with follow-up | 19% / 38% (attributed to Fitness Business Pro 2024, unverified) | third-party | [Rework][rework] |
| Advice on timing | Make the membership offer about halfway through a 2–4 week challenge | third-party advice | [WellnessLiving][wellnessliving] |

**The denominator decides the answer (estimate).** With 200 buyers and 76.5% active, there are 153 graduates and 46 Plus conversions (23% of buyers). At 35% active, there are 70 graduates and 21 conversions (about 10% of buyers). Plus conversion should always be reported per buyer as well as per graduate. The full three-scenario funnel is on [Unit economics](/wiki/b2b2c/economics).

The creator's audience joined for the creator. Loyalty after week 8 may follow the creator, not the app, unless Plus keeps the creator present (estimate).

## Filling 200 seats

There is no credible "X% of followers buy" benchmark. The common "1%" has no traceable source. Creators sell mostly through email.

| Finding | Figure | Date | Label | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kit creator with about 9,000 subscribers | 2–3 low-priced sales per weekly newsletter (about 0.02–0.03% per send); a bootcamp launch made $50K+ mainly through email | Undated case study | company claim | [Kit case study][kit-case] |
| Creator email open rates | 40% average | Undated | company claim | [Kit][kit] |
| Consumers who check follower count before engaging | 17% | 2026 | measured survey (Sprout Social) | [Marketing Dive][md-sprout] |

**Audience needed (estimate, illustrative only).** At 0.5–2% of an engaged email list buying per launch (a rule of thumb we could not source), 200 seats need 10,000–40,000 engaged subscribers. At 0.05–0.2% of social followers, they need 100,000–400,000 followers. Validate these orders of magnitude with pilot creators.

**What a full cohort pays the creator (estimate).** 200 × €9.68 net per ticket = about €1,936 per 8-week cohort, using the per-ticket model on [Unit economics](/wiki/b2b2c/economics). The notes' own rougher figure is about €1,870. For a creator with 50,000+ followers that is small next to selling their own €99–€299 programme at 90%+. It appeals to small creators, who cannot fill 200 seats.

## Recruiting and keeping creators

Evidence is thin. What exists says creators stay where income is meaningful and predictable and where they own the audience.

- Insight Timer pays bonuses for content that brings users back next day, and uses free Live Events as a funnel into teachers' paid content (company terms). [Insight Timer help][it-earn]; [Insight Timer Live Events][it-live]
- Patreon raised its fee to 10% only for new pages and kept existing creators on 5–12% (2025). Unpublishing a page forfeits the legacy rate (company terms). [Patreon help][patreon]; [TubeFilter][tubefilter]
- Maven pivoted from "creators" to "experts" in 2022, which suggests creator-led cohort supply was hard to sustain (third-party, 2022-09-13). [TechCrunch][tc-maven]
- Maven runs a Course Accelerator to recruit and train new instructors (company). [Maven Course Accelerator][maven-acc]

Recruiting 50 creators who can each fill 200 seats is a cold-start problem of its own (estimate). Likely tactics: a few anchor creators on better founder terms, a guaranteed minimum or advance, and demand that Aurelia supplies in-app.

A mid-weight kit (journal, eye mask, tea) at about $5–10 of product plus $9–18 of fulfilment is **$14–28 per box** (estimate). That is at or above the whole €19.99 ticket. All figures are US; EU and UK cross-border shipping adds customs and VAT friction.

## What this means for Aurelia

- **Decide what the 40% buys.** Either Aurelia brings buyers (in-app placement, paid acquisition) or the AI experience must be something no course tool offers. Otherwise offer a creator-favourable split, such as 70/30 or 80/20, and test it.
- **Decide whether the split applies to gross or net.** VAT, card fees and app-store fees come off the €19.99 first. The difference is about €2.30 per ticket for Aurelia (see [Unit economics](/wiki/b2b2c/economics)).
- **Model a creator share of Plus.** Creators will ask for it, and it may be what keeps graduates subscribed.
- **Replace 76.5% with a range.** Plan on 20–50% active at week 8 and report Plus conversion per buyer.
- **Pilot with a few creators before recruiting 50.** Measure seats sold per 1,000 email subscribers, week-8 activity and Plus conversion. Those three numbers decide the model.
- **Make the "box" digital or a separate paid add-on** (for example €49 or more, an estimate). A physical box inside a €19.99 ticket loses money on every buyer.
- **Pay attention to price.** €19.99 is far below premium cohort prices. Testing a higher ticket would help the creator case more than any split change. Creator copy also carries legal risk: see [Claims and regulation](/wiki/b2b2c/regulation).

## Gaps

- Insight Timer's actual teacher percentage and the split on its paid Retreats are not public.
- Calm and Headspace talent fees are private.
- No public example of a platform taking about 40% when the creator brings the buyers and the platform supplies AI.
- No audited completion data for Maven, Reforge or On Deck, and no cohort-size distributions.
- No public completion data for meditation challenges, Insight Timer courses or similar 4–8 week wellness programmes.
- No data on programme-to-subscription conversion for wellness creators.
- No sourced followers-to-email-to-buyers funnel for wellness creators.
- No creator churn data for wellness marketplaces.
- No EU fulfilment benchmarks; the box figures are US.
- **Before quoting:** trace the 76.5% figure to its origin; confirm the Maven corporate 50/50 rate; do not quote the Insight Timer 50% as fact.
