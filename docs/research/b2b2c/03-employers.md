# Employers and insurers
*Part of the [B2B2C research](/wiki/b2b2c). Sources: 49, from the notes in `docs/research/research_notes/Aurelia B2B2C models and plan/`.*

This page covers selling Aurelia through employers, employee assistance programmes (EAPs), insurers and health systems: how these buyers pay, what they demand, and what happened to the companies that sold to them. Research was collected on 2026-11-23. Most findings come from search summaries, not full pages, so single-source numbers are leads to check.

Labels used on this page: **(measured)** is a peer-reviewed or survey measurement. **(company claim)** is a vendor's own marketing or price page. **(third-party estimate)** is an aggregator or analyst figure, not audited. **(trade press)** is a news report. **(estimate)** is our own inference.

## In one minute

- **The realistic price anchor for a content-only app is $50–70 per employee per year.** Calm Teams and Wysa are the only public prices. Clinical-care vendors charge more but bundle coaching and therapy.
- **Uptake is low.** EAP use is about 2–3% of eligible staff. Among employees who enrolled in one digital benefit, only 38% used the self-guided tools.
- **Buyers now demand proof.** Large employers review vendors on dashboards and say vendors must "demonstrate value or face replacement". A pilot with activation and retention data is the entry ticket.
- **Insurers bundle wellness apps as perks, not as reimbursed care.** Kaiser says Calm and Headspace are not covered benefits and may be discontinued at any time. Real reimbursement (Germany's DiGA) needs medical-device status and clinical evidence.
- **The incumbents were repriced hard.** Headspace is being sold for up to $300M after a $3B merger valuation. Pear Therapeutics went from a $1.6B valuation to a $6.05M asset sale because payers would not pay.
- **For Aurelia now:** a self-serve team tier and small pilots, not enterprise or payer deals.

## How employer vendors sell

Employer mental-health vendors almost never publish prices. Sales are by quote. Two structures dominate (Headspace, 2024-07-18, company claim) [Headspace pricing models](https://organizations.headspace.com/blog/evaluating-mental-health-solution-pricing-models):

- **Single fee per employee per month (PEPM):** one fee for all eligible staff covers app access plus a set number of coaching or clinical sessions.
- **Lower base PEPM plus use:** app access at a low PEPM, with coaching, therapy and psychiatry billed per use.

Headspace's example contract runs three years. It says competitors "charge in the double digits per member per month" (company claim, same page).

## What buyers demand

### Proof of value

- Business Group on Health: "Vendors and programs must demonstrate value or face replacement." Its 2025 well-being survey found employers would use dashboards to evaluate vendors, and 100% include mental health in their strategy (measured survey, 2025). [BGH 2025 survey](https://www.businessgrouphealth.org/Newsroom/News-and-Press-Releases/Press-Releases/2025-Employer-Well-being-Strategy-Survey)
- Employers projected a 9% cost increase for 2026 (trade press). [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/payers/employers-brace-9-cost-increase-2026-business-group-health-survey). BGH's August 2026 survey projects a median 9.2% rise for 2027 (measured survey). [BGH 2027 summary](https://www.businessgrouphealth.org/-/media/bgh/images/2027-employer-healthcare-strategy-survey/2027_hcs_survey-es-final.pdf)

Cost pressure plus dashboards means a vendor without usage data is the first one cut (estimate).

**Minimum kit for a first employer pilot (estimate):**

- a security and privacy pack: GDPR (or Indonesia's PDP Law), a data-processing agreement, and an ISO 27001 or SOC 2 roadmap;
- an aggregate, anonymised engagement dashboard, never individual mood data (see [Claims and regulation](/wiki/b2b2c/regulation));
- a crisis-escalation policy.

Direct employer deals for a content-only product can likely avoid US HIPAA "covered entity" duties; health-plan deals pull HIPAA in. This is our inference; confirm with counsel. Clinical claims would push Aurelia towards medical-device rules, which it is not ready for.

## Health systems and the NHS

The NHS route for mental-health apps runs through NICE early value assessments and NHS Talking Therapies. Even Wysa is rated "research only" (regulator, [NICE HTE8](https://nice.org.uk/guidance/hte8/chapter/2-The-technology)). NICE's first rapid healthtech guidance recommended four digital tools for children and young people with mild-to-moderate anxiety or low mood, conditional on DTAC (regulator). [NICE news](https://nice.org.uk/news/article/digital-mental-health-tech-for-children-and-young-people-recommended-by-nice-in-first-rapid-healthtech-guidance)

In the US, Calm Health partners with LifeStance to route users with complex needs to clinical care (trade press). [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/calm-health-lifestance-health-partner-users-complex-mental-health-needs). No sourced evidence was found of health systems paying for general wellness apps at scale.

Health systems are a stage-3 channel at best (estimate). Social prescribing of a wellness app is plausible as an unpaid recommendation, not paid procurement. A clinical route would also make Aurelia a medical device.

From the $3B merger value to at most $300M is a write-down of 90% or more (estimate; the two deals are not like for like).

**Lessons (estimate):**

1. Do not build a model that needs reimbursement before payer-grade evidence exists (Pear).
2. Avoid fixed clinical-labour costs before revenue (Headspace's therapist layoffs).
3. Large buyers are consolidating towards platforms that bundle care. A content-only app should sell cheaply and self-serve, or partner into a platform or EAP as content.
4. Activation and retention data from a real pilot are the currency for every channel.

Sword buying Headspace for its "AI care" positioning is weak evidence that acquirers value AI-personalised delivery.

## What this means for Aurelia

- **First B2B revenue: a self-serve team tier.** Price it near Calm Teams and Wysa ($50–70 per seat per year) and sell without a sales team.
- **Run one or two small employer pilots** and report activation and 30-day retention against eligible staff, not enrolled staff. That number is what every later channel asks for.
- **Build the pilot kit before the first pitch:** privacy pack, data-processing agreement, aggregate-only dashboard with minimum group sizes, crisis policy. See [Claims and regulation](/wiki/b2b2c/regulation).
- **The retreat format can serve employers too.** An 8-week cohort for a team is a bounded pilot with a start, an end and a measurable outcome. Price and claims rules differ from the creator version.
- **Do not pursue insurers, DiGA or the NHS now.** They need clinical evidence, medical-device status, or the scale of a category leader.
- **Do not copy "covered lives".** Report active users.
- How this channel ranks against creators and partners is on the [Plan](/wiki/b2b2c/plan) page; costs and fees are on [Unit economics](/wiki/b2b2c/economics).

## Gaps

- No published PEPM prices for Lyra, Spring Health, Modern Health, BetterUp, Unmind or Calm Health. BetterUp was not researched.
- No published contract sizes or sales-cycle lengths. "6–18 months" is folklore; no source confirmed it.
- No published activation funnels (eligible → registered → active at 30/90 days) from any major vendor.
- No source on what share of employer RFPs require SOC 2 Type II versus ISO 27001, or on SSO, eligibility-file and HRIS integration requirements.
- No primary source on what evidence Kaiser, UHC or Cigna required before bundling Calm or Headspace. The UHC 13M figure needs its primary article.
- No Indonesian employer-benefit penetration data or IDR price points. Indonesian PDP Law and Ministry of Health rules were not covered here.
- **Before quoting:** the Calm Teams $70 is an aggregator figure; the Gallup 2–3% EAP figure is second-hand; Calm's revenue figures are estimates; the Sword–Headspace close date conflicts between sources.
