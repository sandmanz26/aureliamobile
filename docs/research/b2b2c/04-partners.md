# Partners: hotels, gyms, telcos, devices, gifting
*Part of the [B2B2C research](/wiki/b2b2c). Sources: 34, from the notes in `docs/research/research_notes/Aurelia B2B2C models and plan/`.*

This page covers partner channels other than employers and insurers: hotels and airlines, fitness, telecoms and media bundles, device makers, consumer brands and gift cards, universities, and Southeast Asia. Research was done on 2026-11-23 on a small budget, so coverage is uneven. Items marked "search summary" came from a search result, not a fetched page, and need one more check before they go into a deck.

Labels used on this page: **(company)** is a company's own announcement, terms or help page. **(third-party)** is press or an analyst. **(search summary)** was not fetched in full. **(estimate)** is our own inference.

## In one minute

- **Almost nothing measured is public.** No conversion rates, no wholesale prices and no churn after a bundle ends, for any meditation app. Nearly every public item is an announcement. That absence is the main finding.
- **Partner deals that last are the ones where the user pays the app.** Hyatt's trial auto-renews at Headspace's retail price. Singtel bills Calm on the phone bill. Headspace × Oura needs the user's own subscription.
- **Free placements tend to lapse.** American Airlines × Calm, Spotify × Headspace and free Calm on Samsung TVs all ended or were replaced.
- **Big fitness platforms build mindfulness themselves.** Peloton, Apple Fitness+ and Fitbit make their own sessions. Smaller partners are the realistic targets.
- **Two openings fit Aurelia's format:** follow-up after a luxury retreat, which no third-party app serves, and Indonesia, where no global meditation app has a local carrier or super-app deal.
- **Gift codes do not auto-renew** at Calm, so a gifted retreat needs a designed step into Plus.

The Hyatt deal is a paid-acquisition funnel for Headspace: the hotel supplies the trial, the guest pays full price after a month (estimate). No hotel or airline has been shown paying per guest. A modest flat fee, or free placement swapped for acquisition, is likely, but not sourced.

**Retreat aftercare looks unserved (estimate).** Luxury retreats run their own human follow-up and no third-party app. "Your retreat continues as chapters" fits Aurelia's format. Nobody has shown it works, and there is no price to anchor on.

### Two telco models

| Model | Who pays | What Aurelia gets | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Carrier-billed add-on (Singtel) | The customer, on the phone bill; the carrier takes a share | Real payers, small volume | Low; no wholesale price to set |
| Free perk for the whole base (SaskTel) | The carrier, as a wholesale fee to cut its churn | Big reach | "Subscribers" vanish if the deal is not renewed |

This split is our inference. Neither model has a public wholesale price per subscriber.

Spotify × Headspace ending, and Calm licensing content into Spotify instead, suggests a hard bundle with a big platform did not pay enough for either side to renew (estimate; neither company gave a reason).

On public evidence, the channels most likely to create durable payers are those where **the user enters their own payment method** (estimate, from deal design and renewals, not outcome data):

1. carrier-billed add-ons (Singtel);
2. trial codes that auto-renew at retail (Hyatt);
3. data integrations that need the app's own subscription (Headspace × Oura).

Free-for-all placements (seatbacks, in-room TVs, free TV apps, possibly whole-base telco perks) look more like vanity reach. No company has published the conversion behind either side.

## What this means for Aurelia

- **Prefer structures where the user pays Aurelia.** Trial codes that convert at retail, carrier billing with a revenue share, or a data integration. Avoid wholesale licences for a whole customer base: there is no public price to anchor on, and the users disappear when the deal ends.
- **Retreat aftercare is the most natural partner for Retreat-in-a-Box.** A wellness resort or retreat host sells the stay; Aurelia continues it as an 8-week digital programme. It is untested, so pilot it with one host before pricing.
- **Gifting fits the format.** A "gift a retreat" card is a fixed 8-week programme. Design the step into Plus from day one, because Calm-style gifts end without a payment method.
- **Pitch smaller fitness partners, not Peloton or Apple.** Studios and gym groups that cannot make their own content.
- **Wearables: integrate, do not license.** Read sleep or activity data to shape sessions, and sell Aurelia's own Plus. Keep the AI Act limits on emotion inference in mind (see [Claims and regulation](/wiki/b2b2c/regulation)).
- **Indonesia: approach Telkomsel or a super-app** with Indonesian-language sessions and carrier billing. Check local partners first (see Gaps).
- **Measure every partner cohort separately.** The public record has no conversion data by channel; Aurelia's own will be the first. ## Gaps

- No licence fees, revenue shares or trial-to-paid rates for any hotel, airline, telco, device or brand deal.
- No wholesale price per subscriber for any telco or wellness bundle.
- No churn data after a meditation bundle or trial ends.
- No gift-to-paid renewal rates; Headspace's gift terms not checked.
- No per-student university prices or graduate conversion data.
- Not researched within budget: Headspace with Delta, Marriott or Hilton; Aman, Kamalaya or COMO follow-up; Whoop or Garmin; Equinox, Les Mills or ClassPass consumer bundles; Vodafone, T-Mobile, Indosat, XL, AIS or Globe; Grab, Gojek and Riliv partnerships.
- **Before quoting:** the formal end of AA × Calm is unconfirmed; the Spotify × Headspace end dates come from forum moderators; most rows are search summaries.
